## E1: current-board profit split

**Supported mechanism.** `src/PoolBalancer.sol:785–795` withdraws FWA proceeds and applies the current `_inventory.profitShareSum / activeIds.length`. `src/PoolInventory.sol:208–235` changes that sum on every tracked listing addition/removal; a Shape's share is its markup above 10% divided by its listing price. A lower surcharge on replacement listings increases their share. FWA's vendored `StandardPool.sol:1433–1435` snapshots a request base price, and `:1816–1825` credits that stored base to owner proceeds after delivery. Old request proceeds can therefore be split with new listing fractions. The source comment explicitly acknowledges that current-set substitution; it is not a previously undocumented cash path.

**Supplied evidence inspected.** `/private/tmp/fwa-economic-audit-9b45f915/test/V1GasDriftScratch.t.sol` and `/private/tmp/fwa-economic-v1-gas-drift-status.log` show the realistic 18-block transition, five low-fee replacement listings, unchanged policy/settings identity, four fulfilled requests and one expired request. `/private/tmp/fwa-economic-audit-9b45f915/test/PolicyDriftScratch.t.sol` and `/private/tmp/fwa-economic-policy-drift-launch-v2.log` show five fulfilled high-price requests at fixed 25% markup and the exact amounts in the scorecard. The default-V1 test uses 100% markup. The mutable-policy test relists at 25% before those purchases, so this is not the separately accepted in-flight markup-change exception. Both tests compute the purchase-board reference from actual FWA listings and assert actual FWA proceeds equal the old per-request base times fulfilled requests. The earlier one-block fee jump and initial failed setup are not accepted reachability evidence here. The reciprocal 100% mutable-policy underbooking log was identified by the lane report but not independently executed in this review.

**Qualification.** These runs demonstrate drift against O9's stated purchase-board allocation reference; they do not show buyer escrow/backing theft or measured launch-board insolvency. Operating liquid and booked profit belong to the same creator but carry different spending rights, so the transfer can still weaken paid maintenance. Calling this a high economic-outcome violation is defensible by O9, but not a high-severity outsider theft finding. The exact reference arithmetic represents the declared allocation rule, not an independent market valuation of creator entitlement.

**Recommendation / classification.** Treat the source mismatch with O9 and missing transition coverage as code/evidence defects under the present outcome. Derive request-time allocation using supported real dependency data, or propose an explicit, quantified changed allocation outcome. Changing the entitlement/bound or narrowing its promised configuration needs the owner; selecting an implementation that preserves the agreed outcome is engineering work. Do not seek owner approval merely to acknowledge the arithmetic.

**50-round rehearsal.** Can observe default-policy gas drift, actual collections, keeper receipts and current-board allocation on its reached paths. At the proposed 1,500 bps markup and ten-Shape initial targets it does not reproduce either supplied counterexample automatically. No policy replacements are authorized, so it cannot resolve mutable-policy cases. A pass cannot repair or supersede the O9 reference or prove all allowed configurations.

## E5: idle working-call buffer

**Supported mechanism/evidence.** `KeeperPolicyCalls.reserve()` adds the larger of one operating or process budget whenever the pool is open, even with zero owed draws and no missing inventory. `test/PolicyEconomicReview.t.sol:91–155` and `/private/tmp/fwa-economic-zero-demand-buffer.log` show an exact zero-demand round trip, including 0.00368 ETH at 0.5 gwei, reduction to a selected 0.001 ETH cap, funding a replacement policy, unchanged old listing prices, and release on close. Formula at a 0.311 ETH selected maximum and the 50 gwei reference ceiling: 5.3 million gas × 50 gwei + 10 service units × 0.00005 ETH = **0.2655 ETH**. The cap is an extreme allowed policy, not ordinary V1's amount.

**Qualification.** This is intentional working capital, not an accrued keeper debt or confiscated backing. It reverses when the creator disables paid upkeep or closes with no pending work. Reenabling paid upkeep takes the two-day delay; reopening alone does not inherently require that delay if pay was never disabled. With outstanding draws, closing retains their current hold. The report's phrase 'disabling pay or closing releases it, with two days to reenable' should attach that delay specifically to re-enabling paid upkeep.

**Recommendation / classification.** The owner's R46 specifically opposes hypothetical-call locks, while code/documentation intentionally retain one. Resolve this concrete requirement tension. Retaining, shrinking or removing it changes capital access and paid-service resilience; present the measured ordinary amount and extreme envelope, not only the largest number. This is an owner policy choice with implementation consequences, not independent proof that a zero buffer is safe. Accepted delayed restocking does not itself make this design inconsistent.

**50-round rehearsal.** Can measure reserve/claim amounts on its reached configurations. With fixed default policy and overfunded pools it cannot choose the capital-versus-service guarantee or establish exact-minimum resilience. A generic operational pass does not resolve the owner's R46 wording.

## Additional owner-wording correction: creator net metric includes a fixed NFT valuation

`test/scenario/FlowBreakdown.t.sol:893–899` defines:

- `held = claimed + address(balancer).balance + _backingListed() + pool.ownerProceeds()`;
- `cost = deposited + nftsWon * NFT_VALUE`, where `NFT_VALUE = 0.2 ether` for the launch-board scenario;
- `creator_net_gain_wei = max(held - cost, 0)` and loss is the reverse difference.

Therefore the quoted quiet **+0.932648116493624805 ETH** already deducts **0.2 ETH per curated NFT delivered to a winner**, a fixed stated-value contribution proxy. This is not observed NFT market acquisition spending. The harness supplies fresh NFTs via holder transfers after wins; no market purchase price is paid or measured for them. The `held` formula also does not mark remaining curated NFTs to market. Creator transaction gas and actual acquisition/opportunity costs remain outside this metric; keepers, mint fees and protocol/VRF costs affect pool/actor cash according to their actual flows.

`docs/OUTCOME-APPROVAL.md:16,30` currently describes curated NFT 'acquisition/market value' as outside this cash/backing ledger. Refine that wording: **'This includes a 0.2 ETH stated-value deduction per curated NFT won; actual acquisition cost, market/opportunity value and creator transaction gas are unmeasured.'** Do not change the historical raw number, call it pure cash profit, or subtract actual NFT cost again without first replacing/reconciling the included proxy. This is a prose/evidence-description correction, not a change to participant rights or outcome numbers. The separate closeout extra fact at `:755` uses a different `creator_net_gain_wei` formula (`max(claimed - deposited, 0)`); labels must retain their regime context rather than imply one universal valuation model.
