## Ranked security and rights findings

### S1 — High residual purchaser risk, expressly accepted by the owner: hostile curated NFT can be clawed back

Location: OWNER-REQUIREMENTS.md:94-100 (R20), PoolInventory.sol:65-88 (curated listing), and real FWA StandardPool.sol delivery path. A creator can list a collection whose transfer or ownership logic they control. In the executed real-FWA/VRF/Shapes fork test, the player paid **0.017480336629089580 ETH**, including **0.00234 ETH** VRF fee, won the NFT, then the creator's collection seized it. FWA credited **0.014419368218180553 ETH** owner proceeds and no purchaser refund. This is an attacker-controlled listed collection, not a replacement protocol mock. The buyer loses the NFT and payment; FWA treats completed delivery as final. Recommendation if owner changes that accepted risk: constrain eligible curated collections or require enforceable prize custody semantics; that changes creator listing rights and needs an explicit owner decision. ### S2 — Medium purchaser-experience and admission failure: staged prizes count as selectable inventory

Location: PoolBalancer.sol:563-572,702-709; PoolInventory.sol:183-210; OWNER-REQUIREMENTS.md:196 (R49). Balancer increases its tracked prize weight when FWA accepts a listing batch, even if FWA has only **staged** those NFTs behind an unsettled draw. Its purchase gate treats that weight as selectable. On a recent-block real FWA/Shapes/VRF fork, a buyer made one pending draw, the creator raised the target from 20 to 100 small Shapes and deposited 2 ETH, and 16 paid upkeep calls on 16 successive blocks staged 80 replacements before the 30-block randomness timeout. Balancer tracked 100 Shapes and returned admission OK, while FWA had only **20 active Shapes plus one curated NFT**. A second buyer paid **0.015786656757271948 ETH** all-in for a real FWA acquisition that succeeded with that 21-item selectable board; its draw quote was **0.014499656757271948 ETH**. The player still has the FWA prize/refund entitlement and every staged Shape has backing, but the 70%-of-target admission rule accepted a board with just 20% of its target small-Shape weight available. R49 permits temporary depletion while requiring it to be visible and safely admitted. Recommendation: calculate the admission threshold from FWA-active selectable listings, excluding staged items until FWA activates them, and expose active/staged counts in the creator dashboard. The test proves acceptance and the thin board, not a completed second draw or monetary loss.

### S4 — Medium dashboard quote mismatch: direct policy call can understate the fee Balancer will list

Changing a creator-selected mutable policy's surcharge and restocking while old draws wait changes that share without changing the markup or the settings hash. The cause applies even to immutable V1 when its basefee-indexed listing fee falls. A real-FWA default-V1 fork test held markup at **100%**, made five Shape wins, bought five more draws against the high-fee board at basefee **4.95 gwei** and bid **5 gwei**, then advanced **18 separate twelve-second blocks** with each basefee decline within EIP-1559’s one-eighth limit to **0.5 gwei**. Actual owner proceeds were **0.108721948639984132 ETH**; their purchase-board creator share was **0.035694219519450040 ETH**, but Balancer booked **0.037934273133793797 ETH**, **0.002240053614343757 ETH over**, 2.24 times O9’s bound. The documented design also explicitly allows stateful or malicious policies.
