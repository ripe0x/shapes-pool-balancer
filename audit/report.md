# Adversarial audit: Shapes Pool Balancer

## Subject, process, and verdict bounds

This report examines the undeployed `fork-tests` target at `0f017843ec333f499809f751e19017be325448e3` (owner-stated last contract change `31edef60095aecb2f78c863e9f2ab906f1b3c393`). Primary files are `src/PoolBalancer.sol`, `src/BalancerFactory.sol`, `src/interfaces/External.sol`, and `script/Deploy.s.sol`; integrated behavior includes the pinned FWA pool/factory/VRF, Shapes, and ENS reverse registrar. The fork is Ethereum mainnet block **26,382,349**, accessed only through `https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co`. No deployed Balancer was tested and no transaction was broadcast.

Forge/Cast 1.8.1, Solidity 0.8.28 (Cancun, optimizer 200, via IR), Slither 0.11.5, Python 3.11.8, Node 22.19.0 and Vitest 3.2.7 were used. The FWA implementation's pinned runtime hash matched `0x0af01fc1a6ea7ea81fd81004fdb3bd5483872df384d2e4ab4a7599e0d2f0ce02` on the fork. Explorer FWA/Shapes source is partially verified; the exact coordinator implementation and full independent source-to-bytecode reproduction were not completed. The twelve auditor-hour cap was approved. The evidence files are editable in the shared workspace. Discovery exclusion was **prompt-level only**, not process-enforced; both discovery lanes reported reading no excluded document/test/history or sibling finding before sealing at 01:59–02:00 UTC on 2026-11-11. Later tests and source reconciliation are **informed**. Calibration is uncalibrated, structural gate not passed, and independent human technical review is absent. See [run record](run-record.md), [call ledger](call-ledger.md), and [integration assessment](integrations/assessment.md).

The validated outcomes below are bounded to their named actors, fork state, and test assertions. F1 shows an outsider-triggered NFT seizure **only when the creator listed a collection with that public backdoor**; durable attacker profit was not demonstrated. No universal profitable keeper cycle was demonstrated. The audit remains incomplete against the schema-2 path-by-path gate and the gaps below.

## Findings, highest severity first

### F1. A hostile collection can remove an NFT while its FWA listing remains purchasable (Medium, conditional)

**Site:** `src/PoolBalancer.sol:544-562` (`admissionStatus`), `:655-681` (`_checkpoint`), `:977-996` (`_curatedTotals`). Balancer checks FWA's active listing count and weight totals, but those totals remain unchanged when a collection moves a token out of FWA through its own backdoor. FWA's eventual delivery failure and buyer refund are integrated behaviors the Balancer relies on. This requires a hostile ERC-721 collection with out-of-band transfer authority; the PoC does not apply to an ordinary immutable collection.

**Concrete local transactions:** (1) Creator lists token 123 from the test's `SeizableNFT`, a separate normal curated NFT and one Shape, then opens and checkpoints. (2) An unrelated EOA calls the collection's public `seize(pool, stranger, 123)`. The NFT owner becomes `stranger`, yet FWA listing status remains active, its active count still matches Balancer's tracked count, and `admissionStatus() == 0`. (3) A funded buyer contract pays the FWA quote for one draw. The test supplies a valid VRF word selecting the ghost listing; mainnet buyers are **not** shown to control that word. FWA marks delivery pending because it no longer owns the token. (4) Upkeep reopens admission for other inventory. After seven days, the buyer calls `refundFailedDelivery` and `withdrawAcquisitionRefund`; FWA returns the full acquisition fee. (5) Creator recovery of the ghost NFT reverts, while unrelated curated withdrawal, Shape unwind and surplus claim succeed.

**Impact:** the creator loses custody of the token to the collection's `seize` destination. Its market value is unmeasured; the test's creator-declared base was 0.1 ETH, which is **not** a valuation. In the selected-draw branch at 1 gwei, the buyer's contract balance ends exactly **0.00234 ETH** below its starting balance, the nonrefundable VRF fee; the acquisition fee is refunded after a seven-day liquidity lock. Transaction gas is additional and unmeasured. The stranger pays seizure gas; repeated public seizure means durable ownership and attacker profit were not proved. The test establishes admission of a ghost listing, not that every random draw selects it or that the whole pool is blocked.

**PoC:** `audit/controller/GhostInventory.t.sol`; run `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/controller forge test --match-path audit/controller/GhostInventory.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`. Exit **0**, `[PASS] testOutOfBandCuratedSeizureLeavesAdmittingGhostListing` in `audit/controller/GhostInventory.final.out`; an earlier shorter passing witness is preserved as `GhostInventory.out`. The independent [informed challenge](challenge/ghost-review.md) reviewed the original witness; its continuation amendment reviews the added post-refund/exit assertions.

**Suggested fix:** constrain curated collections to ones whose pool-owned tokens cannot be seized/burned by third parties, and state that custody/admission depends on those collection rules. A fresh `ownerOf` check can detect some changes but cannot reliably prove honest custody for arbitrary hostile collection code within FWA's 100k purchase-hook stipend.

### F2. A hostile curated collection can permanently prevent withdrawal of its listed token (Medium, conditional)

**Site:** `src/PoolBalancer.sol:387-389` (`withdrawCurated`), `:397-401` (`recoverCuratedNFT`). FWA transfers the collection's token during withdrawal/recovery, so the collection can reject it. The owner requirement says no outsider can permanently block creator exit; that guarantee cannot hold for an arbitrary curated ERC-721 that later refuses transfers.

**Concrete local transactions:** (1) Creator approves and lists token 123 from the test's one-way-freeze ERC-721 into its real forked FWA pool. (2) An unrelated EOA invokes the mock's permissionless `freeze()`; the trace measured 43,492 gas for this call, excluding transaction overhead. (3) Creator closes the Balancer. (4) Creator calls `withdrawCurated(1, creator)`; FWA reaches the collection's `safeTransferFrom` and receives `Frozen()`, reverting atomically. The token remains at FWA with active listing status. There is no unfreeze method in this mock. The attack requires a collection with that authority and behavior; ordinary immutable ERC-721 transfer semantics are not covered.

**Impact:** one creator-supplied token is inaccessible through withdrawal or post-refund recovery for as long as its collection rejects transfer. Its ETH market value is not established. In the buyer continuation, a buyer drawing that token waited seven days, received the **full FWA acquisition-fee refund**, and retained a loss of only the separate VRF service fee (plus gas outside the test's contract balance). Balancer upkeep, another real Shape purchase, unrelated curated withdrawal, Shape unwind and creator claim of all remaining liquid ETH succeeded. Thus the proof does **not** establish whole-pool exit failure or loss of the buyer's acquisition-fee refund.

**PoC:** `audit/controller/HostileExit.t.sol`; run `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/controller forge test --match-path audit/controller/HostileExit.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vvv`. Exit **0**, `[PASS] testFrozenCuratedNFTBlocksCreatorWithdrawal`; the saved trace is `audit/controller/HostileExit.out`. Continuations: `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/challenge forge test --match-path audit/challenge/HostileContinuations.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv` and `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/challenge forge test --match-path audit/challenge/HostileBuyer.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`; each exited **0**, one passed, zero failed ([buyer ledger](challenge/hostile-buyer.md)).

**Suggested fix:** explicitly restrict curated collections to immutable, transferable behavior if guaranteed withdrawal is a product promise; otherwise narrow the exit guarantee and disclose the collection-specific freeze risk before listing. The Balancer cannot force a hostile ERC-721 to transfer.

### F3. A creator-controlled NFT callback can leave an admitting pool with stale listing markup (Low)

**Site:** `src/PoolBalancer.sol:284-299`, `:331-363`, `:955-975`. `_pullCurated` computes a price before calling the creator's collection. If the collection is itself the creator, its `transferFrom` or `approve` callback can call unguarded `configure`. With no previously active listing, `configure` does not set `repricing`. The outer listing records the old price and later upkeep can checkpoint using FWA's actual totals, without comparing each item to the current markup.

**Concrete local transactions:** (1) A creator contract that is also an ERC-721 collection configures 0% markup. (2) It calls `listCurated` for an NFT with base 0.1 ETH. (3) During the collection callback, it calls `configure` with 100% markup. (4) Outer listing records and FWA lists at 0.1 ETH. (5) Upkeep checkpoints; `repricing == false`, `admissionStatus() == 0`, although current config requires 0.2 ETH. The batch variant lists two equal-base NFTs at 0.1 and 0.2 ETH after the callback changes markup between items.

**Impact:** a documented fixed-markup/accounting invariant fails while purchases are admitted. A separate FWA-pull callback fork witness bought the affected NFT: the live acquisition fee was **0.034999999999999999 ETH**, while directly repricing that one listing to the current 100% markup in a test snapshot raised FWA's *hypothetical* quote to **0.038181818181818181 ETH**, a **0.003181818181818182 ETH** difference. The snapshot bypassed Balancer bookkeeping and did not execute a purchase at the counterfactual price. The buyer contract paid the live quote plus VRF fee and received the NFT. This is a **buyer discount relative to the current config**, not a demonstrated buyer or creator loss; the creator itself controlled the callback and config. No third-party theft was established. The [independent informed review](challenge/markup-buyer-review.md) accepted these limits.

**PoCs:** `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/lane-a forge test --match-path audit/lane-a/Callback.t.sol --match-test testCreatorCollectionChangesMarkupDuringFirstListing --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`: exit **0**, one passed (base and stored price 1 ETH versus configured 2 ETH in that variant). Independent `audit/lane-b/MarkupCallback.t.sol` used `approve` callback: exit **0**, one passed ([sealed lane B](lane-b/discovery.md)). `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/challenge forge test --match-path audit/challenge/BatchMarkupCallback.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`: exit **0**, one passed ([challenge review](challenge/review.md)). `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/controller forge test --match-path audit/controller/MarkupBuyer.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`: exit **0**, one passed in `audit/controller/MarkupBuyer.final.out`; this added FWA-pull callback, counterfactual quote snapshot, actual funded buyer payment and delivery.

**Suggested fix:** reject configuration changes during listing/asset-mutating callbacks or recalculate/revalidate the listing price and mark repricing after every external collection call before checkpointing.

### F4. Balancer status 0 can coexist with a VRF funding rejection after local FWA expiry (Low, conditional availability)

**Site:** `src/PoolBalancer.sol:544-562` (`admissionStatus`), `:604-636` (`_sync`), integrated FWA VRF router coverage in `FWACloneVRFService.prepareRequests`. Balancer does not query router coordinator-outstanding liabilities or liquid subscription funding. FWA's local timeout and Balancer's reconciliation do not clear those liabilities.

**Concrete fork sequence:** (1) Open/checkpoint with one curated NFT and one Shape. (2) A buyer sends a five-draw FWA batch at 1 gwei, paying **0.0117 ETH** in five VRF service fees. (3) The PoC advances 40 blocks *without a coordinator callback* and FWA processes all five as expired; their local listing IDs are zero and owner proceeds zero. (4) Keeper calls `performUpkeep`, creating a fresh checkpoint with `admissionStatus() == 0`. (5) A sixth one-request purchase is attempted at 1 gwei. The Balancer hook permits it, but the router reverts with exact `InsufficientVrfCoverage()`; the sequence remains five. On this pinned fork the router retains five coordinator liabilities, needs a 0.45 ETH subscription top-up for one more, and has about 0.117341005115044987 ETH after the proposed sixth fee, a roughly 0.332659 ETH shortfall. A second *five*-request batch has a 2.123298994884955013 ETH shortfall at 1 gwei and crosses the tested coverage boundary between 182 and 183 gwei.

**Impact:** the attempted sixth purchase loses transaction gas only; its purchase and service fees revert. A separate funded-buyer continuation measured the first five payments and withdrew the entire acquisition-fee portion after local expiry. Its buyer contract balance ended exactly **0.0117 ETH** below its starting balance, the five VRF service fees (gas excluded). Balancer may pay a gas-only keeper reward at step 4; that amount was not measured in this PoC. An arbitrary buyer can initiate the first batch but cannot alone force forty blocks without Chainlink callback. The continuation delivered one late authenticated callback and a next one-request purchase then succeeded at the same gas price. This is **not** proof of a permanent outsider-caused halt.

**PoCs:** `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/controller forge test --match-path audit/controller/VrfBacklog.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`: exit **0**, `[PASS] testExpiredLocalBatchesCanLeaveRouterCapacityExhausted`, logs five requests and 11,700,000,000,000,000 wei service fees in `audit/controller/VrfBacklog.final.out`. `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/controller forge test --match-path audit/controller/VrfRecovery.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`: exit **0**, one passed in `audit/controller/VrfRecovery.out`, with purchaser refund and late-callback retry assertions. `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/integrations forge test --match-path audit/integrations/VrfBoundary.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`: exit **0**, one passed in `audit/integrations/VrfBoundary.final.out`; a prior incorrect `expectRevert` harness run is retained as `VrfBoundary.out` and does not support the claim. Independent [VRF challenge](challenge/vrf-review.md) accepted the original conditional state and narrowed the actor/loss claim; the [recovery review](challenge/vrf-recovery-review.md) accepted the later refund/retry assertions within the pinned fork. A further [zero-commitment continuation](integrations/vrf-zero-commitment.md) passed (`FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/integrations forge test --match-path audit/integrations/vrf-zero-commitment.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`, exit **0**, one passed): an outsider's reconcile reverted while the real coordinator commitment was nonzero; after a **fork-only `vm.store`** made that commitment zero, permissionless reconciliation released one liability and a sixth purchase succeeded. It does not show an outsider can zero a real commitment.

**Suggested fix:** distinguish Balancer inventory admission from end-to-end FWA purchasability in API/UI; if status 0 must mean an executable FWA purchase, incorporate a router capacity check that accounts for request count and current transaction fee, or expose a separate FWA quote/status. Do not equate local expiry with coordinator liability release.

### F5. A no-op creator configuration exempts purchase replacement work from the edge-credit cap (Low)

**Site:** `src/PoolBalancer.sol:284-299` (`configure`), `:604-628` (`_sync`), `:843-860` (`_payKeeper`). Any creator `configure` call sets `creatorWork = true`, even when config and plan are identical. `_payKeeper` then skips the `incentiveCredit` limit for later inventory work. The call is allowed while a FWA draw is outstanding.

**Concrete fork transactions:** (1) Creator funds, lists a 0.005 ETH-base curated NFT and one 0.01 ETH Shape, opens and checkpoints; `creatorWork == false`. (2) Buyer submits one real FWA draw. (3) Before settlement, creator calls `configure` with the **same** max price, odds, markup and plan; it changes no intended inventory or price but marks `creatorWork = true`. (4) The test supplies a valid VRF word selecting and delivering the Shape. FWA records acquisition fee **0.008049999999999999 ETH**, owner base proceeds and an actual delivered edge of **0.000383333333333333 ETH**. (5) An unrelated keeper calls `performUpkeep`, which reconciles and refills the drawn Shape, then receives **0.00098252875 ETH**. Its event attributes **0.000386023 ETH** to the gas component, leaving an **0.00059650575 ETH** inventory incentive. The purchase-derived `incentiveCredit` remains **0.000383333333333333 ETH** instead of being spent.

**Impact:** the keeper's inventory incentive exceeds this draw's delivered FWA edge by **0.000213172416666667 ETH**. The full reward is paid from Balancer ETH; FWA's edge goes to FWA buyback. This violates the stated *purchase-caused incentive cap* in this creator-assisted sequence, while remaining below the **0.0165 ETH per-call maximum**. The buyer received the Shape. The creator authorizes the no-op call and ultimately controls Balancer ETH, so this is not outsider-only theft or a proved coalition profit after transaction gas and asset values. The test asserts the keeper's balance rises by the returned reward, the Shape is refilled, and the actual FWA proceeds split equals the computed credit.

**PoC:** `audit/controller/CreatorWorkCredit.t.sol`; run `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/controller forge test --match-path audit/controller/CreatorWorkCredit.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv`. Exit **0**, `[PASS] testNoOpConfigureExemptsPurchaseRefillFromIncentiveCredit` and the numeric ledger above in `audit/controller/CreatorWorkCredit.out`. An initial compile attempt failed from a missing test-only `Vm` import and was corrected before the passing run. The independent informed [challenge](challenge/creator-work-credit-review.md) accepted the numbers and actor bounds. `docs/SPEC.md` also says creator-started work earns the full incentive, so the owner must settle whether that exception was meant to reclassify an **already-issued** purchase after a no-change creator call.

**Suggested fix:** determine reward eligibility from the actual operations that need reimbursement, not a sticky boolean set by any creator call. A no-op configuration should not exempt purchase-caused replacement from `incentiveCredit`; define how mixed creator and purchase work shares the cap.

### Secondary findings (bounded local client/keeper proofs)

These were examined after primary discovery because budget remained. The single exact command was `npx vitest run --config audit/secondary/vitest.config.ts audit/secondary/Flows.test.ts audit/secondary/KeeperLoss.test.ts`: exit **0**, four passed, zero failed. The executable files and detailed mocked call ledgers are in [secondary review](secondary/report.md). No browser wallet, FWA profile server, or chain transaction was exercised.

- **S1, Low, app/src/tx.ts:14-18 and app/src/App.tsx:123-124:** `prepareWrite` simulates factory `createBalancer` as account A but drops `account` from the returned write params. If the connected wallet switches to B before `writeContractAsync`, wagmi sends as B. The local test asserts A was used for simulation/estimate and B for the write action. A resulting real factory call would fund and assign the new clone to B; no transaction or ETH loss was executed. The at-risk deposit is the user's chosen `msg.value`, plus gas, under that race. **Fix:** retain the simulated account in the write request and revalidate wallet/chain/address immediately before submission.
- **S2, Low, app/src/App.tsx:156-163:** when connected account changes A→B, `loadMine` replaces `mine` with B's pools but leaves nonempty `selected` pointing to A's pool. The executed callback test confirms this state. A later permissionless write could spend gas on the stale pool; an owner of both pools could perform a valid creator write on the wrong one. No such transaction or ETH loss was executed. **Fix:** clear or reselect `selected` whenever the connected account or valid pool list changes, and bind pending reads to the selected address.
- **S3, Low, app/src/App.tsx:127-143 and :650-665:** `useTx.send` catches an approval failure and resolves; `PoolPage.publish` then POSTs the profile anyway. The local test injects wallet rejection and observes one stubbed POST plus a stubbed “FWA accepted” result. Real server acceptance and unauthorized publication were **not** demonstrated; no ETH is at stake in this test. **Fix:** propagate the failed transaction/receipt state and submit only after a successful approval receipt for the exact pool-page digest.
- **S4, Low, keeper/keeper.ts:42-46 and :282-301:** the configured `MAX_LOSS_WEI` checks estimated cost at current basefee, not eventual receipt cost. In the local fee-path proof, a zero-reward 155,000-gas call passes the default 0.0002 ETH allowance, then permitted basefee rises and a replacement can be included at **0.000208311523515 ETH**, exceeding that allowance by **0.000008311523515 ETH**. This is a possible receipt outcome, not a mined receipt. The operator docs already call the setting estimate-based, so this is a limit of the requested *absolute* cap, not a contradiction of that narrower written policy. **Fix:** label it an estimate allowance or define and enforce a conservative maximum-fee × gas-limit budget before sending; cancel/replacement behavior still needs a defined worst-case policy.

## Missing or unsafe requirements

1. **Who owns unsolicited contributions:** `depositETH`, plain ETH reception, and direct NFT transfers provide no contributor ledger or refund. The current spec grants creator recovery; the fork PoC transfers a stranger's 1 ETH plus NFT to the creator. If third parties may fund or accidentally send assets, this is an explicit economic/right transfer that should be stated in product-facing terms, or the requirement changed.
2. **What odds floor means across a batch:** a sealed fork test admitted five draws with a positive curated floor, selected the only curated NFT first, and left four subsequent selections with zero curated inventory. The current rule tests the initial checkpoint's *combined* odds. If each paid draw is promised a minimum, the batch rule is unsafe; if initial odds are intended, state that precisely.
   A mixed-batch campaign also observed FWA's ordered-settlement price-drift refund after earlier deliveries changed the live quote. The acquisition fee becomes a purchaser credit, while the separate VRF fee remains spent. The owner should state whether a paid batch promises five delivered NFTs or permits fewer deliveries with nonrefundable service fees.
3. **Keeper incentive source:** the full FWA edge on a delivered purchase goes to FWA buyback in the inspected pinned implementation. `incentiveCredit` caps a payout from **Balancer ETH**; it is not fee income to Balancer. An informed fork ledger found a negative outsider cycle, but the source of funding should be explicit to creators.
4. **All-in buyer cost and purchase availability:** `maxDrawPrice` intentionally excludes the VRF fee. A 0.012075 ETH admitted FWA fee carried an additional 0.00234 ETH VRF fee in the fork quote. `admissionStatus == 0` expresses Balancer policy, not a guarantee that FWA cooldown, factory pause, or router funding allows `acquire` now. These distinctions should be explicit wherever the status/ceiling is shown.
5. **ERC-1271's universal wording:** `isValidSignature` returns magic for an approved digest and *any* signature bytes to *any* caller. It cannot know how an external verifier interprets that same digest. It can reject other digests, including independently formed Permit2/Seaport typed-data digests absent a cryptographic collision, but a universal ban on every other EIP-191/712 consumer is stronger than the interface can establish without constraining consumers and message semantics.
6. **Hostile collections:** listing arbitrary curated ERC-721s and guaranteeing live custody or withdrawal are incompatible when a collection can seize a listed token or permanently reject transfers. The rights should be conditioned on collection behavior, as F1/F2 show.
7. **Reserve across creator changes:** the literal claim that `claimSurplus` can never leave ETH below `requiredReserve()` needs a state qualifier. `requiredReserve()` is recalculated from the *current* creator plan and Shapes fee. A creator can claim against one plan and then configure a costlier plan, increasing the computed requirement without returning ETH; the checkpoint becomes invalid and purchases close. The focused fork `FOUNDRY_TEST=audit/controller forge test --match-path audit/controller/ReserveReconfig.t.sol --fork-url https://mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co --fork-block-number 26382349 -vv` exited **0**, one passed ([output](controller/ReserveReconfig.out)); it asserts the new requirement exceeds cash and `canPurchase` is false. This is a funding obligation for reopening, not an admitted underreserve purchase. State the guarantee for an unchanged plan and fee at the end of the claim, and define the required funding step after reconfiguration or fee growth.
8. **Mixed creator and purchase work:** `docs/SPEC.md` grants a full keeper incentive for work started by a creator action, while the owner rule caps purchase-caused incentive by delivered FWA edge. F5 shows the rules conflict when a no-change creator call happens after an issued purchase. Specify whether the already-issued draw's reconciliation/refill remains credit-limited and how an upkeep call with both causes allocates measured gas and fixed incentive.
