## Findings, ranked by consequence

### Economic

**E1 — High — executed: capital withdrawal can outrun a delivered 100 ETH prize.** [PoolBalancer.sol](../src/PoolBalancer.sol) `claimSurplus` at 479 calls `withdrawableSurplus` at 703; `_reserveInput` at 723 uses `_inventory.activeShapes`. That count drops only in `_onDrawn` at 657 during upkeep. On the real FWA/Shapes fork, a buyer received a 100 ETH-backed Shape through the real VRF service. Before upkeep, the tracked count still included it, `requiredReserve()` was **0.00508 ETH**, and the creator claimed **100 ETH** of capital. After upkeep, reserve became **100.00542 ETH** against **0.7719518974 ETH** operating cash at block 26,473,847: a **99.2334681026 ETH** restoration shortfall. Reproduced at historical block 26,382,349 as well. The buyer's delivered backing and FWA refund rights were not taken; the harmed requirement is R67's full inventory restoration withdrawal reserve and R49's accurate depletion/shortfall accounting during the reconciliation interval. The creator needs their normal withdrawal right; this is a timing bypass of its stated reserve. **Smallest fix:** expose only creator credit as withdrawable while `lastIssuedSequence() > reconciledSequence`, and reject capital claims in that interval; keep credit-only claims free of policy, fee, reserve and inventory reads. Alternatively, count actual delivered-but-unreconciled deficits exactly. Update the dashboard's withdrawable amount in the same state. Witness: `test_deliveredHundredEthPrizeCapitalClaimableBeforeReconciliation` in [UninformedFork.t.sol](UninformedFork.t.sol), [recent-finding.log](recent-finding.log). No repair was authorized or made.

**E2 — Medium — reasoned: an unknown wallet transfer can be allowed to repeat.** [App.tsx](../app/src/App.tsx) at 263–279 records a nonce from `getTransactionCount(..., 'pending')` **before** opening the wallet, then stores the returned transaction hash with that earlier nonce. [tx.ts](../app/src/tx.ts) at 76–83 treats an unknown hash as resolved once the account's latest nonce exceeds the stored nonce. If the wallet submits the ETH transfer at a higher nonce, the read RPC cannot find that pending hash, and a different lower-nonce transaction mines, the app clears its pending-transfer guard while the first transfer can still settle. A second click may send another irrevocable contribution under R73. **ETH impact:** up to the amount of the duplicate transfer per retry, e.g. **10 ETH** for a repeated 10 ETH deposit; no actual duplicate was broadcast or observed. The source comment at 76–78 acknowledges the higher-nonce case, and the shipped test uses a stored nonce matching the submitted one. **Smallest fix:** after submission, bind the guard to the actual transaction nonce when available; while that nonce or final receipt is unknown, keep the transfer unresolved and do not infer completion from the pre-wallet nonce. This is a reasoned wallet/RPC ordering path, not a reproduced transaction.

**E3 — Medium — executed model/fork mismatch: the operating simulator rejects standard Shapes-only pools.** [sim/fund.ts](../sim/fund.ts) at 109–117 always requires a curated listing. With 20 small Shapes, 1 ETH initial capital, 15% markup, 0.5 gwei basefee and five requested draws, the model reports **0 draws, 5 refusals, 0 player spend**. A real-FWA fork with the same board, opening capital and gas context completed five sales: **0.07570875 ETH** paid by the buyer excluding transaction gas, **0.065975 ETH** owner proceeds, **0.00329875 ETH** FWA edge and **0.006435 ETH** one-way VRF fees. The exact day schedule and keeper gas model were not matched step by step, so these figures establish the mode error, not full trajectory parity. Direct on-chain loss is **0 ETH**; the model suppresses at least these sales and receipts in this reachable mode, making its Shapes-only economic projections unusable for R74–R75 and the `shapes_only` regime. **Smallest fix:** add admission mode to the scenario input, apply the curated gate only in curated-required mode, and run a stepwise fork comparison for each mode. Witnesses: [ModelCheck.ts](ModelCheck.ts), [model-check.log](model-check.log), and `test_fiveStandardShapesOnlySalesAgainstModelZeroSales` in [UninformedFork.t.sol](UninformedFork.t.sol).

## Things that don't make sense

1. A 100 ETH winner can already hold the asset while the withdrawal reserve still counts it as listed. The pool can truthfully report a roughly 100 ETH restoration need only after its creator has taken the replacement cash.
2. A stale nonce can clear the UI's safety guard before an irreversible ETH contribution is known to be final.
3. The Shapes-only model refuses every draw even though standard-mode FWA accepts them. A balanced simulated ledger says nothing about the missing sales.
4. R73 makes a contributor's 10 ETH immediately claimable by the creator if it is surplus. This is the owner's stated rule, but the UI must make that economic transfer plain before contribution.
5. R36 still names a factory-admin `maxPrizes` control, while the running code has a hard 1,000-prize cap and no factory setter. The later supersession does not clearly settle which rule the owner wants.
