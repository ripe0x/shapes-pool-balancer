## Security findings, ranked by consequence

**A1, high, executed: a missing pending-hash read releases a higher-nonce transfer before it mines.** `App.tsx:273–277` saves the pending nonce *before* opening the wallet. The wallet may select a higher nonce. `tx.ts:79–84` uses the saved lower nonce when the pending hash is unavailable, and releases the guard when any transaction with that lower nonce mines. The test sent 0.01 ETH to real mainnet WETH at nonce 2 on a pinned fork, mined an unrelated nonce 0, and verified the first transfer remained pending. Its external hash read then failed with `TransactionNotFoundError` (a permitted off-chain service failure; all successful nonce and balance responses came from the node). `transferUnresolved` returned `false`. A second 0.01 ETH transfer at nonce 1 and the original at nonce 2 both mined; the sender's WETH balance increased by exactly **0.02 ETH**. At a 5 ETH launch deposit this permits **5 ETH extra** to move to the Balancer. Under R73 that contribution is irrevocable, carries no sender refund right, and available surplus may be claimed by the creator. Exposure is bounded by wallet funds, not by this guard. Smallest local fix: never use the pre-wallet nonce to prove resolution of a hash whose actual nonce was not observed; retain the block until the transaction or a trustworthy wallet record reveals its nonce and its consumption is confirmed.

**A2, high, storage behavior executed; wallet submission and lying RPC reasoned: local storage and tab races bypass the guard entirely.** `App.tsx:252–255` treats missing, malformed, cleared, blocked or failed local storage as no pending transfer. Two tabs can both read no record at `App.tsx:269`, then enter their wallet prompts before either saves at line 277. The additional probe executed the candidate's exact `loadPending`/`savePending` helpers in two separate contexts against Node's real Web Storage implementation: both read no record, and the second save replaced the first hash. After clearing storage, both returned no pending transfer. A reload preserves the record only while browser storage survives. A lying RPC can also report `blockNumber != null` for the pending hash and release immediately at `tx.ts:83`, without a receipt or canonical-block check. Distinct chain IDs and case-folded account/contract addresses are correctly separated by `transferKey`; this does not close the shared-storage or RPC-trust paths. One extra 5 ETH transfer has the same R73 impact as A1; the function imposes no transfer-size cap. Smallest practical fix: synchronously reserve a cross-tab intent before opening the wallet, record and verify the actual nonce, and fail closed on an unverified outcome. The absolute promise across *cleared* storage or an RPC that supplies false success cannot be enforced by this browser-only record; that part needs a trusted shared transaction source or an on-chain idempotency rule, or the product promise must be narrowed by the owner.

## Things that don't make sense

- The source comment at `tx.ts:76–77` explicitly acknowledges that a higher wallet nonce can clear the guard early, while the UI presents the guard as refusing a second unconfirmed ETH transfer.
- A browser-only local-storage record cannot guarantee the requested behavior after storage clearing or against a lying RPC. That mismatch needs an explicit product/architecture decision before this can be represented as an absolute protection.
