## System map and economic pass

Derived from source before running the gate: creator deposits ETH into Balancer reserve and contributes curated NFTs; Balancer mints backed Shapes/Draw Packs and pays one-way mint fees, then lists assets in FWA. Buyers pay FWA's all-in quote: draw proceeds plus a one-way randomness fee. FWA either delivers a Shape/Pack/NFT, or records a player refund/credit. FWA owner proceeds flow into Balancer. The first 10% of Shape markup replenishes reserve while short; surplus goes to the creator. Balancer pays useful keeper calls from creator-controlled ETH, and closeout unlists/redeems backing, claims surplus and withdraws FWA proceeds. Each bucket was traced through sale, settlement, restock, closeout, or refund. Gate destinations for quiet/limits-at-max/max-then-idle were stable within signed bounds: creator claimed 40.2%/36.3%/36.3% of inflow; winner backing 27.5%/31.7%/31.7%; listed backing 18.1% each; reserve change 5.7% each; Shapes mint fee 2.7%, randomness 2.5%, FWA-held pending/refunds 2.1% each.

The code change's running cost is queue processing in paid `performUpkeep`; creator funds the reward, while keeper retains storage refunds. At launch board scale, 180 draws, the signed no-processor measurement gives **31,039 gas/draw × 0.55 gwei × 180 = 0.003073 ETH** retained refunds, or **31,105 × 5 gwei × 180 = 0.0279945 ETH**. In the 540-draw no-processor regime that is 0.0839835 ETH, 4.2% of creator net as signed. Burst regime: 51,591 gas/draw; worst single call 182,980. These refund amounts are incremental, not the total keeper pay. The configured keeper reward for a useful call caps at **0.0018875 ETH** at .55 gwei and **0.01635 ETH** at 5 gwei (3.2M measured gas + 50k overhead + .0001 incentive). One processing call can serve two draws; adversarial timing can make it serve one. Processing skips restock in that call, adding a later useful call. The gate's total keeper pay includes setup and play, so it cannot isolate only the incremental queue cost without a controlled paired run. No smaller incremental claim is made here.

## Security findings (ranked by consequence)

### S1 — Shipped fork helper charges the test contract, not the named buyer — **executed, new**

`test/utils/ForkBase.sol:116-119` calls `vm.prank(who, who)` before evaluating the `pool.inventoryVersion()` argument to `pool.acquireBatch`. Foundry's one-shot prank is consumed by that intervening external view call; `acquireBatch` is then invoked by the test contract. My fork attack copied that exact call order and checked both balances. **Output:** named buyer pays `0`; harness pays **0.024397386592787382 ETH** for one real draw at .55 gwei. The earlier version of my refund attack failed with `player paid real quote: 0 != 24397386592787382`; moving the view before the prank made the actual buyer pay and allowed refund accounting to pass. This affects shipped tests using `ForkBase.buy`, including `ProcessQueue`, and weakens their claimed actor and payer model. It does **not** establish an on-chain loss or invalidate the gate's separate buyer-payment helper, which computes the version first and checks balance loss. **Smallest fix:** capture `inventoryVersion` before `vm.prank`, assert named buyer balance falls by full quote, and rerun affected focused tests. **Evidence:** `evidence/RCFresh.t.sol.txt`, `test_shippedForkBaseBuyMakesHarnessPayInsteadOfPlayer`, exact command `forge test --match-path test/RCFresh.t.sol --gas-limit 100000000000 -vv`; output `evidence/fresh-final.log` (10 passed; specific log `draw paid by harness wei: 24397386592787382`), with original failed assertion in `evidence/fresh-discovery-failure.log`. No tracked fix was made under the audit's no-edit rule.

### S2 — Non-gas FWA processing revert could leave a ready queue head — **reasoned, previously identified caveat**

`performUpkeep` catches `processAcquisitions(2)` failure. If the returned gas exceeds its starvation threshold, it excludes failed gas from keeper billing and continues to collect/reconcile/restock. With a new FWA non-gas failure at the queue head, the draw could remain waiting while a different action gets paid; repeated keepers cannot advance it through this path. **No concrete trigger was found in the deployed FWA code traced here:** expiry and reserved-batch settlement were exercised and succeeded; real FWA's notifier isolates downstream callbacks. This is a dependency-change/liveness risk, not an executed exploit. At launch quote measured by S1, five waiting draws would represent up to **5 × 0.024397386592787382 = 0.12198693296393691 ETH** gross buyer payments pending, plus sale pause duration; actual recoverable draw portion depends on FWA's state and is not quantified as loss. **Smallest fix:** surface a distinct processing-failure signal with operational recovery/alert, and verify against any future FWA implementation before adoption. The bounded argument and its known exception are in `keeper/keeper.ts`; no throwaway test demonstrates a non-gas failure because no real trigger was found and mocks were forbidden. This is deliberately marked reasoned.

## Things that don't make sense

1. The shipped helper says it buys for a player but charges the test harness. Tests that inspect ownership yet not payer balance can pass while modeling the wrong actor.
2. `FlowBreakdown` sometimes advances proceeds with a direct `collectProceeds` call that the bundled keeper does not make. The tested boundary did not create a material final balance difference, but the gate's actor schedule differs from production.
3. The owner calls the design self-sustaining, yet a signed large-win regime loses 0.167222488 ETH and reaches a 1.051699243 ETH reserve shortfall. R15/O5 expect a creator top-up or dropping the missing prize. The word “self-sustaining” needs that condition attached.
4. The 400,000-gas catch threshold is a measured assumption about current FWA proxy/notifier behavior and six-listing reservations. It is not a contract-enforced proof for a future external implementation.
