## Trace correction and observed outcome

The retained `audit/controller/VrfBacklog.out` trace shows the **first** `acquireBatch(..., count=5)` succeeded (line 362), paid `0.0117 ETH` in VRF service fees, and issued five Chainlink requests. The test advanced 40 blocks, locally expired and processed those requests, then ran Balancer upkeep. `Balancer.admissionStatus()` again returned `0`. The **second** five-draw batch reached `FWACloneVRFService.prepareRequests(5)` (line 733) and reverted `InsufficientVrfCoverage()` (line 790). Its `RequestsPrepared` event in the reverted frame shows five already-outstanding coordinator requests plus five newly prepared requests. The event and tentative accounting are rolled back; they identify where the attempt failed, not committed service state.

The successful first batch and failure of the second are consistent with the [router's verified source](https://eth.blockscout.com/address/0x7D93Bd9de5dc121992EA4A59E61B2519Fa1Ed65d?tab=contract), `src/userowned/FWACloneVRFService.sol` lines 257–280 and 376–465. The service requires `minimumSubscriptionBuffer + (totalPrepared + totalCoordinatorOutstanding) × maxFulfillmentCostWei` in the native Chainlink subscription. `prepareRequests` adds the new fee to the service's liquid ETH, raises `totalPrepared`, and tops up the subscription; if the needed top-up exceeds its liquid ETH, it reverts. Moving a prepared credit to coordinator-outstanding during request issuance does not reduce the coverage requirement. A local FWA expiry reduces FWA's `unsettledAcquisitionCount` but **does not** clear the service's Chainlink-outstanding count. Only an authenticated coordinator callback or the router's commitment-based lost-callback reconciliation can release it (`FWACloneVRFService.sol` 475–579). The pool's source likewise keeps `unfulfilledVrfCount` after local expiry (`StandardPool.sol` 249–266, 1512–1516, 1579–1642).

## Balancer admission and impact

`PoolBalancer.admissionStatus()` checks its own enabled/checkpoint/sequence/inventory/Shapes fee/liquid reserve/acquisition price conditions (`src/PoolBalancer.sol` 544–562). It does not query the router's outstanding liabilities, subscription balance, liquid treasury, or purchaser gas price. FWA calls `canPurchase` before `prepareRequests`; in the trace it returned `true`, then router funding failed. Thus **status 0 is a valid Balancer-policy admission but can be stale or incomplete as an end-to-end claim that FWA can accept a purchase**. FWA's source correctly reverts the attempted purchase before issuing new requests when coverage is insufficient.

The directly demonstrated harm is **purchase unavailability despite Balancer status 0** and gas spent on a reverted attempt. The second batch's ETH payment, including acquisition and VRF fees, rolls back on revert; no second-batch escrow or service fee is lost. The first batch's five VRF fees, `0.0117 ETH` at 1 gwei in the controller trace, are nonrefundable even though FWA locally expired those acquisitions; its acquisition fees become purchaser refund credits, not automatic transfers. Balancer upkeep can spend a keeper reward to reconcile that expired batch and reopen its checkpoint while the router still cannot fund the next batch at ordinary gas prices. The router's per-pool cap would independently reject new requests once ten coordinator requests remain outstanding. These observations establish an availability/economic mismatch under the stated outage sequence, not asset theft or permanent unavailability. A later authenticated callback, successful lost-callback reconciliation when the coordinator commitment is zero, new service funding, or sufficiently high purchaser fee can change the state.

## Focused fork reproduction and limits

`audit/integrations/VrfBoundary.t.sol` uses the existing `ForkBase` against the pinned public mainnet fork. It created a Balancer, listed one curated NFT and one 0.01 ETH Shape, opened and check-pointed, bought five at 1 gwei, advanced 40 blocks without VRF fulfillment, processed FWA's local expiries, and ran upkeep.
