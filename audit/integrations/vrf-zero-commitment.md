## Source rule and authority

`FWACloneVRFService.reconcileCoordinatorRequest(requestId)` (`:539-554`) is `external nonReentrant` with **no owner/operator restriction**. It requires router status `CoordinatorOutstanding`, reads `s_requestCommitments(requestId)` from its current coordinator using a fixed-size `STATICCALL` (`:557-569`), and reverts if that commitment is nonzero or the read fails. Only with a successful **zero** result does it set status `CoordinatorCallbackLost`, decrement the pool/global outstanding counters (`:572-579`), and emit `CoordinatorRequestReconciled`. It creates no replacement randomness, pays no caller reward, and does not itself withdraw a buyer refund. `requiredSubscriptionBalance()` is `minimumSubscriptionBuffer + (totalPrepared + totalCoordinatorOutstanding) × maxFulfillmentCostWei` (`:261-263`); the next `prepareRequests` calls `_ensureSubscriptionCoverage` (`:457-465`).

The real coordinator commitment for the first request remained **nonzero** after FWA locally expired five requests on the pinned fork. An unrelated EOA therefore could not reconcile merely by waiting for FWA expiry; its call reverted `CoordinatorRequestStillPending`. The test used Foundry `vm.record` to locate that request's coordinator storage slot, then **`vm.store` to set it to zero only in the ephemeral fork**, modeling a coordinator commitment consumed without a callback stored by the router. This privileged test intervention is not an on-chain action available to the EOA. The exact real-world transition that could leave a zero commitment while the router still reports `CoordinatorOutstanding` was not executed or proved from the coordinator implementation.

## Focused fork sequence and asset ledger

The new [test](vrf-zero-commitment.t.sol) creates a local Balancer/FWA pool on the pinned fork, lists one 0.1 ETH curated NFT and one Shape, opens and checkpoints it, then uses a funded buyer contract to pay for five FWA requests at 1 gwei. After forty blocks without a coordinator callback, FWA locally expires all five, the buyer withdraws its acquisition-fee refund, and Balancer upkeep restores status 0. Router outstanding remains five. The test asserts that a sixth purchase reverts `InsufficientVrfCoverage` without sequence advance or buyer balance debit.

## Bounded implication for F4

The router's **permissionless zero-commitment path can release one counted liability and restore one-request capacity** in the tested fork state, without new treasury funding. This narrows F4 against a claim that the coverage rejection must remain permanent once the coordinator commitment is gone. It does **not** weaken the demonstrated F4 interval: local FWA expiry alone left the real coordinator commitment nonzero, Balancer status 0, and a rejected purchase. The test did not execute a real Chainlink proof, establish a naturally occurring lost-callback/zero-commitment state, measure mined gas, or model other pools' simultaneous liabilities. The exact remaining reachability question is whether and when the pinned coordinator can delete a commitment while the router has not stored its callback; absent that condition, an outsider's reconciliation reverts.
