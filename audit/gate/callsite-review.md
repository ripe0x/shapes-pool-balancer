# Informed external-call inventory review

**Scope.** This maps all 75 `external_calls` IDs in `audit/source-inventory.json` to the frozen `PoolBalancer.sol` call sites and also maps the six IDs in `audit/factory-inventory.json`. The current source SHA-256 values match the inventory: `PoolBalancer.sol` `bc2cf8d26848bf17f919d8fe0187057d9f7f720770ac38acc49904ef28e2c94d`, `BalancerFactory.sol` `1f995d6cc4761498aa782efa861999c636461d79f9347550cd277ad2d51ebaae`. This is a source-site classification, **not proof of reachable-path completeness or behavioral safety**. It uses the earlier pinned mainnet external-source review at block 26,382,349 where FWA, Shapes, and ENS behavior matters; no new fork execution was needed for this mapping.

**Inventory interpretation.** Eight IDs at lines 506/518 refer to four *internal* Solady `LibString`/`SignatureCheckerLib` computations, each represented twice by the inventory. The two IDs at line 442 represent one ETH `CALL`, as do the two at line 860. The remaining IDs denote external EVM call sites; a single source site may execute repeatedly in a loop. Thus 75 IDs map to **65 distinct external EVM call sites** and four internal library computations, without implying 65 independent runtime paths. In the tables, `P` is the FWA pool created in `initialize`, `F` is immutable `FWA_FACTORY`, `S` immutable `SHAPES`, `E` immutable ENS registry, and `C` a creator-supplied ERC-721 collection. `view` means the Solidity interface call is a `STATICCALL`; the callee can revert or return adversarial data but cannot persist state changes in that call tree.

## Callback authority at these sites

The asset-changing entry points `listCurated`, `listCuratedBatch`, `updateCuratedPrice`, `withdrawCurated`, `recoverCuratedNFT`, `recoverUntrackedNFT`, `collectProceeds`, `claimSurplus`, `unwind`, `performUpkeep`, and `recoverRefundedShape` have Solady `nonReentrant`. A callback during one of those paths cannot enter another guarded path. It can still reach `receive`/`depositETH` (which add ETH), public getters, `canPurchase`/`admissionStatus`/`requiredReserve`, and `onERC721Received` (pure). The unguarded role functions `transferCreator`, `acceptCreator`, `configure`, `open`, `close`, `retire`, ENS name functions, and pool-page approval functions are reachable **if the callback sender independently has creator or pending-creator authority**. This can arise from a creator-chosen contract/recipient or later role transfer; the guard alone does not prohibit it. `initialize` sets `creator` before its first external call, so recursive initialization fails its one-time check.

The FWA pool, factory, Shapes, ENS registry/registrar and arbitrary ERC-721 collection are distinct authorities. The mutable FWA factory and ENS registry determine some later call targets/configuration; the creator chooses collections, NFT IDs, bases and some payout recipients. FWA can invoke nested NFT transfer and notifier callbacks; the review of those deployed integrations is in `audit/integrations/assessment.md` and `audit/integrations/informed.md`. At `claimSurplus` the creator-selected recipient receives an all-gas ETH `CALL` and can reject it, reverting the claim. At keeper payment the arbitrary keeper receives a 100,000-gas attempt; Solady falls back to forced ETH via an ephemeral `SELFDESTRUCT` sender if the attempt fails. At Shape mint/withdraw the Balancer's ERC-721 receiver is pure, and at redemption/FWA proceeds its ETH receiver has no logic. These observations do not eliminate collection, FWA or role-aligned callback effects.

## Limits and review disposition

This is an informed classification of mechanically inventoried source calls, not a new behavioral campaign. It does not prove that the inventory found every low-level, compiler-generated or nested external call, that any target is nonmalicious, or that callback paths are exhausted. The highest-consequence mutable edges remain creator-selected ERC-721 collection methods, Shapes mint/redeem and approval, FWA inventory/settlement/proceeds methods, and arbitrary ETH recipients. For those, the ledger states the preconditions and rollback/guard behavior visible in source; independent asset-identity and adversarial callback execution remains a separate audit question. No production code, existing tests, broadcasts or external state were changed.
