## Source reasoning that controls the classification

1. **The 24 library alerts have no target caller.** `PoolBalancer` invokes `LibString.toHexString(address/uint256,bytes)` for one pool-page message, `SignatureCheckerLib.toEthSignedMessageHash(bytes)` for its digest, and `SafeTransferLib.safeTransferETH` / `forceSafeTransferETH` for ETH. `BalancerFactory` invokes `LibClone.cloneDeterministic` and `predictDeterministicAddress`. The reached hex encoders call their own `toHexStringNoPrefix` overloads, not `LibBytes` search/slice helpers, escape/case/URI routines, or `LibString.eqs`. The target does not call SignatureCheckerLib's signature/6492 verifiers, LibClone.implementationOf, or SafeTransferLib.permit2. Each flagged function is `internal`; no external actor can directly invoke it through these deployed contracts. Importing an entire Solady file made Slither analyze functions with no incoming target call. A later source change adding such a call must reopen its record.
2. **The high `_fillDeficit` alert is real ordering, but its exploitability is open.** `SHAPES.mintBatch{value:cost}` at `PoolBalancer.sol:815` runs before `p.listNFTs(batch)` at `:826` and `items`/active Shape writes at `:829-834`. `performUpkeep` is guarded; the inspected Shapes mint calls Balancer's pure `onERC721Received`, and FWA pulls fixed Shapes. This limits ordinary callback reachability. A source/runtime-complete proof of the fixed dependencies and creator-aligned unguarded callbacks is absent, so the high alert stays unresolved. The detailed row states the asset ledger and deciding trace.
3. **Five target rounding alerts are about discrete FWA math and plan choice.** `_curatedTotals` and `_shapeTotals` floor each Shape price and inverse weight before multiplying count/price. This appears intended to match FWA's `floor(1e36/price)` listing weights; the exact boundary equivalence was not proved. `_feasible` floors the weighted mean before applying the edge, so a one-wei boundary could select or reject a proposed plan. `_checkpoint` and `admissionStatus` each read live FWA `acquisitionFee()` and compare it with `maxDrawPrice`, limiting a pure `_feasible` rounding discrepancy from directly admitting an above-ceiling fee. The remaining risks are wrong odds/plan choice or upkeep liveness, and require the listed FWA boundary comparisons.
4. **The keeper arithmetic alert is bounded by source math.** At `:844-848`, `measured ≤ 3,200,000` and `gasUsed = floor(4×measured/5) ≤ measured`. With nonnegative capped `price`, `gasUsed×price` cannot exceed `measured×price`. This says nothing about the separate incentive, actual receipt gas, or bundled self-dealing. At `:859`, `reward == 0` only skips a zero-wei transfer and event after balance capping. The five uninitialized alerts name `uint256` locals, which Solidity initializes to zero; their broader reserve/fee formulas remain separate questions.
5. **Ignored FWA tuple fields encode dependency assumptions.** Curated refund recovery, Shape refund recovery, reconciliation, and Shape withdrawal use selected listing/acquisition fields and ignore others. The omitted fields are not by themselves lost return values from an asset transfer, but the target relies on FWA listing identity, ordering, status, and escrow semantics. The relevant rows remain unresolved until those exact FWA invariants and hostile variants are checked. The known one-way-frozen curated NFT finding remains a separate confirmed conditional withdrawal block.
6. **ENS return values affect identity state.** `setENSName` and `clearENSName` ignore the registrar's returned `bytes32` and emit the requested name without resolver readback. The pinned registrar was probed once, while the ENS registry may select another registrar later. No ETH or NFT authority is granted in those calls, but a false or redirected reverse record remains unresolved.
