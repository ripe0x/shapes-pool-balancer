## Claim and evidence-domain decision

**Claim accepted within stated bounds:** on the pinned fork, if five FWA requests receive no coordinator callback for forty blocks, they can expire locally, Balancer upkeep can write a status-0 checkpoint, and the next one-request FWA purchase at the test gas price can revert `InsufficientVrfCoverage()` before issuing a sixth request.

The final test asserts all five first-batch request IDs have `listingId == 0` and status `3` after `vm.roll(block.number + 40)` and `pool.processAcquisitions(5)`, asserts `unsettledAcquisitionCount() == 0` and `ownerProceeds() == 0`, then calls Balancer upkeep. Immediately before the next purchase it asserts `admissionStatus() == 0`. Its `try/catch` requires the exact `InsufficientVrfCoverage()` selector, sets `coverageBlocked`, and finally asserts `lastIssuedSequence() == accepted == 5` and admission remains 0. The passing `.final.out` reports **1 passed, 0 failed**, `requests issued before coverage block 5`, and `nonrefundable VRF fees wei 11700000000000000`. Therefore no sixth request was accepted in this execution. The final output is `-vv` and does not contain a call trace, but the assertions are specific to the state and revert selector.

The retained earlier failed run was an **unhandled second five-request batch**. Its trace independently reaches FWA `acquireBatch`, `canPurchase` returning true, then router `prepareRequests{value: 0.0117 ether}(5)` and an exact `InsufficientVrfCoverage()` revert. The tentative `RequestsPrepared` event in that reverted frame is rolled back; it is not committed state. That run failed as a Foundry test, so it supports the call-path observation only, not the final test's one-request assertion. The informed `VrfBoundary.t.sol` independently tests the same backlog: five router coordinator-outstanding requests after local expiry, a 182-gwei five-request attempt reverting with the same selector, and a 183-gwei attempt succeeding under its pinned balance/configuration. It does not prove ordinary live gas prices will stay in either range.

**Claim narrowed:** this is conditional availability failure and misleading end-to-end interpretation of Balancer status 0, **not** an outsider-alone ability to halt the pool, permanent shutdown, theft, or loss of the second attempted payment. The test deliberately advances forty blocks without any Chainlink callback. An arbitrary buyer can submit the first batch but cannot, merely by buying, force the coordinator to delay or omit authenticated callbacks. A competing builder would need the ability to censor/delay those callbacks across the relevant deadline; the test does not demonstrate that power. The source shows authenticated callbacks release `totalCoordinatorOutstanding` before attempting delivery, while FWA local expiry does not. The router also permits commitment-based `reconcileCoordinatorRequest` only when the coordinator commitment is zero. If a commitment stays pending and no callback arrives, that reconciliation route cannot be used.

## Asset and cost ledger

At 1 gwei, the first five-request quote was `0.121472727272727270 ETH` total. The earlier trace shows the buyer's FWA call forwarded `0.0117 ETH` to `FWACloneVRFService.prepareRequests(5)`, matching five × `0.00234 ETH` service fee. `ForkBase.buy` makes the same address both payer and named purchaser. The five requests then expire without drawing any listed NFT; FWA owner proceeds are zero. Under the inspected FWA pool source, the **named purchaser** receives refund credits for the acquisition-fee portion, which must be withdrawn separately, while the VRF service fee is nonrefundable. The test does not assert the exact refund-credit amount or execute withdrawal, so the supported direct cash-loss claim is the buyer-paid **0.0117 ETH VRF fee**, subject to that source behavior; it does not show the acquisition-fee portion permanently lost. The buyer also pays transaction gas, unmeasured here.

The failed sixth request sends no new FWA or router fee because the purchase reverts atomically; its caller pays only reverted-transaction gas. Balancer itself does not pay the VRF service fee. Balancer upkeep after expiry can pay a gas-only keeper reward from Balancer liquid ETH even though no owner proceeds accrued, but this test does not record the reward or a before/after Balancer cash ledger. A creator/buyer/keeper identity combination and net economics cannot be inferred from the fee log alone.

The saved router source computes `requiredSubscriptionBalance = minimumSubscriptionBuffer + (totalPrepared + totalCoordinatorOutstanding) × maxFulfillmentCostWei` and reverts if the needed subscription top-up exceeds service liquid ETH (`FWACloneVRFService.sol` lines 257–280, 376–405, 457–466). At the pinned values reported in `vrf-boundary.md`, after the first five requests locally expire the coordinator subscription is at `2.6 ETH` with five outstanding and service liquid ETH is about `0.115001005115044987 ETH`. A sixth single request at 1 gwei requires the subscription to reach `3.05 ETH`, a `0.45 ETH` top-up. Its new `0.00234 ETH` service fee leaves a roughly **0.332658994884955013 ETH** service-liquid shortfall. This calculation explains why even one request fails in the final test; it depends on those pinned balances, no callback or other treasury funding, and unchanged router settings. The previous `vrf-boundary.md` calculation for a *second five-request batch* has a larger `2.123298994884955013 ETH` shortfall at 1 gwei and is a different attempt size.

## Recovery routes and unresolved extent

- An authenticated Chainlink callback releases one router outstanding liability even if the pool already locally expired that acquisition. The test does not execute a late callback, so actual post-expiry recovery timing remains unmeasured.
- If a coordinator commitment becomes zero without the router receiving its callback, anyone may call `reconcileCoordinatorRequest(requestId)` to release that liability. If the commitment is still pending, the function reverts `CoordinatorRequestStillPending`; merely waiting for FWA local expiry is insufficient.
- The router accepts ETH from anyone and `topUpSubscription()` is permissionless; new purchase fees also fund coverage. Under the pinned six-request calculation, roughly `0.333 ETH` of additional service funding would be needed at 1 gwei before that single request can pass coverage, absent any callback or other state change. This is a service subsidy, not a Balancer-reserve requirement. A sufficiently high buyer gas price also raises the service fee, but makes the purchase itself more expensive. Once ten coordinator requests from this pool remain outstanding, the router's per-pool cap independently blocks an eleventh until a liability is released; funding alone cannot bypass that cap. Factory/router governance can pause or change future settings within its authority, but no governance action was exercised here.
- **Material remaining test gap:** the final PoC does not assert purchaser refund credit/withdrawal, actual payer balance delta, Balancer keeper reward, or a late callback/reconciliation recovery sequence. These are needed before claiming a complete loss/recovery amount or attack profitability. It also does not model normal coordinator fulfillment latency or establish a live likelihood of forty-block delay. The exact next deciding test, if the lead needs broader economic or liveness claims, is one fork sequence that records payer/refund/Balancer balances before and after expiry/upkeep, then delivers a late coordinator callback (or zero-commitment reconciliation) and retries one purchase at the same gas price.

**Disposition:** accepted within the pinned no-callback, five-expired-request, 1-gwei state; narrowed against outsider-alone, permanence and total-fee-loss claims. The prerequisite and remaining asset/recovery questions stay open. This is not a conclusion about safety or release readiness.
