## Participant and value model used for discovery

- Creator supplies curated NFTs and working ETH, chooses plan, price, odds, recipient and successor, then receives pool owner proceeds and surplus. Creator may be a contract and may be adversarial. There is no depositor balance ledger. An arbitrary ETH/NFT sender receives no contractual claim on the Balancer.
- Purchaser pays the FWA quote plus the VRF service fee and may be different from payer. FWA gives the named purchaser the NFT, refund and delivery rights. A draw can settle/refund after the Balancer closes. A pool purchase is admitted once per checkpoint, including a batch of up to five draws.
- Shapes backing stays in the Shapes contract while the NFT is listed or held by a purchaser. The Balancer's reserve is liquid ETH, distinct from backing and FWA purchase escrow. On mint, backing plus mint fee leaves the Balancer; on redemption, backing returns without the mint fee. FWA owner proceeds become Balancer liquid ETH only after FWA delivery and withdrawal.
- Keeper can perform upkeep or recover a refunded Shape and receives bounded ETH reimbursement to its caller address. A keeper can also be the creator, a contract or a block builder.

## Executed counterexamples and surfaced questions

### B1. Creator-controlled collection can leave an admitting checkpoint with a stale curated markup

`audit/lane-b/MarkupCallback.t.sol` (SHA-256 `fcdad43f392f8cbdb24966bb87fbfe739dbcfdff6988a110f1142bbd3fd84cf9`), `testCreatorCollectionChangesMarkupDuringListing`: first execution failed at setup with `NotOwnerNorApproved()` because the test approved as the wrong caller; corrected and rerun. Final run: **exit 0, 1 passed, 0 failed**. On the pinned fork, a creator that is itself an ERC-721 collection invokes `listCurated` at zero markup. Its `approve` callback invokes unguarded `configure` as creator to set 100% markup before `_recordCurated`. Since there were no active listings yet, `configure` does not set `repricing`. FWA and the Balancer item both retain the 0.1 ETH listing price, the saved base is 0.1 ETH, current markup demands 0.2 ETH, and `admissionStatus()` returns OK after upkeep. `listCurated`'s `nonReentrant` does not block `configure`.

This demonstrates a reachable configuration/listing invariant mismatch, not quantified buyer or creator loss. FWA's visible price, fee and current weight totals are still used in checkpoint feasibility. The creator controls both the old and new settings, so extra authority over someone else's assets has not been established. **Open:** whether dashboard or owner requirements promise that current `markupBps` determines every active curated price. If yes, review affected buyer displays and consider a non-reentrant configuration boundary or post-callback price validation. The test covers this callback and no other curated NFT mutation.

### B2. A positive curated odds floor does not hold for every draw in one admitted batch

`audit/lane-b/OddsWithinBatch.t.sol` (SHA-256 `a67ab56e03af4e548608ece032490d8f2101cce4411e793af1e85bd8374119f7`), `testCuratedOddsFloorAppliesOnlyBeforeBatch`: **exit 0, 1 passed, 0 failed**. With one curated item, five 0.01 ETH Shapes, and `minCuratedOddsBps = 100`, the checkpoint admits a five-draw purchase. Forked FWA VRF settlement selects the curated item first and four Shapes thereafter, so the final four draws have zero curated inventory and zero chance of a curated item. The test asserts each acquisition is fulfilled, exactly one listing ID is curated, four are Shapes, and the buyer owns the curated NFT. It proves a concrete within-batch state transition, not a probability distribution.

**Open owner-rule question:** Does the odds floor mean only the initial snapshot for the batch, or each paid draw? The local specification says "min combined curated probability" and states admission once for a batch, so the former may be intended; the name and user-facing interpretation could suggest the latter. If per-draw odds are promised, this is a rule failure affecting buyers and creators. FWA's buyer-visible inventory and request slippage may limit surprise, but that was not established by this test.

### B3. Unsolicited contributors lose ETH and NFT recovery rights to creator

`audit/lane-b/ContributionRights.t.sol` (SHA-256 `b3a8f4cc76c0ee5007a2a39a51be113985ecd3f32ef27e87f1ac50c1ba294615`), `testUnsolicitedETHAndNFTBecomeCreatorRecoverable`: **exit 0, 1 passed, 0 failed**. A stranger deposits 1 ETH and safely transfers a mock ERC-721 to the Balancer; while closed, the creator calls `recoverUntrackedNFT` and `claimSurplus`, obtaining both. This is explicitly permitted by the specification and code, so it is a **rights-design exposure**, not an implementation defect. Direct contributors have no withdrawal or refund path. The same right applies to direct transfers of valuable Shapes. The test does not cover assets still held in FWA or a purchase refund.

### B4. Keeper incentive accounting uses the whole FWA edge despite owner sharing it

`_sync` computes `incentiveCredit = priceEscrowed * current edgeBps / (10000 + edgeBps)` for delivered draws. The FWA settings documentation says the edge is split half to owner and half to protocol, and the owner also receives base expected value: <https://v1.fwa.fun/docs/custom-settings>. If creator and buyer are the same actor, the owner half returns to that actor, so the actor's net non-VRF edge expense is only the protocol half. The comment's statement that self-purchase incentives are bounded by the "edge paid to FWA" is therefore not a full net-cost bound. Also `edgeBps` is read on reconciliation, not purchase; governance can change it between those points. **Unresolved impact:** actual keeper incentives, VRF service fee, Shape outcome, gas and subsequent surplus must be combined in a forked net-asset ledger before claiming a profitable cycle or reserve drain. FWA governance is not assumed attacker-controlled. No such profit witness was run in this lane.

### B5. Admission status omits FWA purchase cooldown and shared availability

The Balancer's `admissionStatus()` can return OK immediately after listing/repricing while FWA still enforces its inventory cooldown, and it checks `globalWithdrawOnly` in upkeep but not in `admissionStatus()`. FWA's standard-pool documentation confirms a post-change purchase cooldown and factory-wide purchase controls: <https://v1.fwa.fun/docs/custom-standard>, <https://v1.fwa.fun/docs/custom-settings>. This is an availability/display mismatch; FWA itself reverts an unavailable purchase. A caller may spend gas on a doomed transaction if it treats Balancer status as the whole purchase preflight. A separate forked purchase-revert assertion and product usage review would decide materiality. No loss from the Balancer's custody was demonstrated.

## External-call asset/authority ledger and callback reachability

The ledger covers every external call family in the primary contract. `nonReentrant` covers `listCurated`, `listCuratedBatch`, `updateCuratedPrice`, `withdrawCurated`, `recoverCuratedNFT`, `recoverUntrackedNFT`, `collectProceeds`, `claimSurplus`, `unwind`, `performUpkeep` and `recoverRefundedShape`. During their callbacks those entrypoints revert at the guard. Unguarded callable entries remain `depositETH`/`receive`, views, `onERC721Received`, `transferCreator`, `acceptCreator`, `configure`, `open`, `close`, `retire`, ENS setters, page approvals/revocation. The last nine require creator or pending-creator authority; a creator contract can exercise them from its own collection/recipient callback. `initialize` is unguarded but the clone's nonzero creator after the first call blocks replay. A call to `canPurchase` is static and cannot mutate state.

Most value-moving entries are guarded, but the creator-control entries remain reachable in callbacks as B1 shows.
