## Findings ranked by consequence

### Economic findings

1. **High, executed: two 0.5 ETH wins leave the top prize unstocked despite the opening reserve.** On the real FWA/Shapes fork at block 26,382,349, the 47-prize, 1.7 ETH backing board with a 0.5 ETH top prize was opened with a 5 ETH deposit, 25% markup, 0.5 gwei basefee and 0.55 gwei bid. The creator claimed surplus each day. Force top-prize wins on draw attempts 60 and 61, with upkeep after each. The first is replaced; after the second, `performUpkeep` cannot fund another 0.5 ETH prize. In the 180-attempt, 30-day run, 119 keeper passes ended with a deficit and the final board still lacked 0.5 ETH of backing. Maximum reserve shortfall was 1.051673572548063379 ETH; final fund held 0.260548739633175672 ETH against `requiredReserve` of 1.0347 ETH. This violates owner requirement 4, although the low-inventory admission gate still allowed the remaining sales. **Smallest fix direction:** reserve enough liquid ETH for the specified consecutive top-win run, with a tested claim limit and explicit recovery policy; re-evaluate whether the creator profit floor delays refilling. Exact new reserve amount needs sizing against the chosen win streak and board.

2. **Medium, executed: billed-gas basis differs from keeper reward basis.** The eight-regime asset gate measures cumulative keeper pay above its estimated billed gas plus its 50,000-gas allowance and 0.0001 ETH flat reward of 0.0014804317 ETH in the five-at-once regime, 0.00076343575 ETH in closeout, and 0.00004541075 ETH in the gas-spike regime. `PoolBalancer._payKeeper` prices pre-refund `gasleft()` delta; the fork harness estimates post-refund billed gas. This is money from the creator-funded pool. A focused isolated keeper test also records positive per-call margins, including 0.000597985 ETH for a five-Shape replacement at 5 gwei. These are fork billing models, not mined transaction receipts. **Smallest fix direction:** make the owner's reward rule explicit in billed versus pre-refund terms and bound the excess over actual billed gas; if the stated gas-plus-small-flat outcome is literal, reduce the refund-sensitive allowance or base pay through a different keeper policy. A contract cannot read the final transaction gas refund directly.

3. **Medium, executed: useful post-close reconciliation can pay zero.** On the real FWA fork with a real BAYC listing, ten Shapes, five draw requests, 0.5 gwei basefee and a 0.55 gwei bid: creator `close()`; `claimSurplus()` drains the Balancer; wait 31 blocks; FWA `processAcquisitions(5)` expires every request, giving zero `ownerProceeds`; keeper `performUpkeep()` advances `reconciledSequence` from 0 to 5 but returns 0 and leaves the keeper wallet unchanged. The candidate's own reward formula would pay **at least 0.0001275 ETH plus measured gas** at this bid, or 0.00035 ETH plus measured gas at a 5 gwei bid. The exact transaction gas was not separately measured. This violates owner requirement 1 for a useful call, and makes rational outside keepers skip that closeout path. It does not by itself prove assets are stranded; the creator can still do the work. **Smallest fix:** keep an encumbered amount for outstanding reconciliation through `close` and `claimSurplus`, or obtain an explicit owner exception for unpaid post-close work. The shipped closeout test has later fulfilled draws whose proceeds replenish the drained balance, so it does not reach this path.

4. **Medium, reasoned: the stated per-winner restock-fee payer is not how FWA charges.** FWA charges one inverse-price-weighted expected-value draw price before the random outcome, then credits the same snapshotted owner base price whether the winner receives a Shape, a Pack or a curated NFT. A curated-NFT winner therefore pays part of the Shape restock fees embedded in the board price, even if no Shape is won; a Shape winner does not individually repay that Shape's full listing fee. On the launch board, at 0.5 gwei basefee and 0.55 gwei bid, removing the restock fees from every Shape/Pack listing while keeping the same inventory changes the independently computed FWA owner price from **0.022009891993130840 ETH** to **0.021010969848467187 ETH** per fulfilled draw. This is an approximately 0.0009989221 ETH price contribution for *every* draw, including an NFT win. The outstanding 55 Shape fees total 0.038225 ETH at this gas; at the 5 gwei pay cap they total 0.2585 ETH. **Smallest fix:** decide whether the requirement means aggregate fees funded by all buyers, then state and measure that rule. Literal winner-specific repayment would need a different FWA settlement design; this Balancer cannot enforce it through listing prices alone.

5. **Medium, reasoned and price-tested in part: 5 gwei operation may price out players.** A fork call confirmed `restockFee() = 0.0047 ETH` per Shape at 4.95 gwei basefee and a 5 gwei bid. With the live 0.001 ETH Shapes mint fee and 15% markup, one 0.01 ETH Shape would list for 0.0172 ETH, and a homogeneous FWA draw would cost 0.01806 ETH **before** the 0.0117 ETH VRF service fee and buyer transaction gas. For the 47-prize launch board at 25% markup, integer arithmetic from the verified FWA weight and fee formulas gives a freshly listed 5 gwei quote of **0.040743929569407318 ETH** including VRF, versus 0.024397386592787382 ETH at 0.55 gwei; expected prize backing is about 0.0170 ETH at the cap. The eight-regime run did execute 45/45 planned draws at the cap, but its board was partly listed earlier at lower fees; its observed first quote was 0.034810386592787382 ETH. Player willingness has no approved numeric threshold, so economic attractiveness remains an open owner outcome. **Smallest fix:** set a maximum acceptable all-in draw price and net expected return for the chosen launch board, then choose board, fees or gas policy to meet it.

6. **Medium, executed: permissionless FWA processing lets a keeper split one ready batch into five paid reconciliations.** `test/KeeperPay.t.sol::test_splitCallsAreBoundedByProcessedDraws` creates five real FWA draws that time out, then compares `processAcquisitions(5)` followed by one upkeep against five rounds of `processAcquisitions(1)` followed by upkeep. At 0.5 gwei basefee and 0.55 gwei bid, the single call pays **0.00020229505 ETH**; five calls pay **0.0008240985 ETH**, an extra **0.00062180345 ETH** from the creator's Balancer. The caller need not have bought the draws; FWA processing and Balancer upkeep are permissionless. Every split call does reconcile work, so this stays within the literal per-call small-reward rule, but it disproves the candidate's anti-splitting premise in `PoolBalancer` comments. If repeated over 36 five-draw batches (180 draws), the extra is about **0.022385 ETH** at this measured mix, including extra gas reimbursement. **Smallest fix:** if that cost is acceptable, budget one paid call per draw and correct the premise; if it is not, make the fixed incentive and reimbursed overhead independent of permissionless FWA processing granularity.

7. **Medium, reasoned: the candidate's creator and player figures omit costs it already knows.** At `ec5259c`, `app/src/App.tsx` labels `ev.drawPrice` as “Players pay” and the Overview says restocking fees are paid “to keepers”; `playerView` separately adds `d.vrfFee`, while the contract actually pays keepers from creator ETH even for NFT wins and expiries. On the launch board at 5 gwei, the prominent player amount would show **0.029043929569407318 ETH** while FWA's all-in draw payment is **0.040743929569407318 ETH**, a **0.0117 ETH** omission before wallet gas. At 0.55 gwei the omission is 0.001287 ETH per draw, or **0.23166 ETH across 180 draws**. The planner's “You keep per draw” is gross markup and its monthly projection does not subtract keeper costs or simulate the observed large-win reserve gap. The candidate also hardcodes FWA VRF cost as 2.34 million gas in the UI, though the verified service allows its fee settings to change. **Smallest fix:** display the live FWA total quote with explicit fee components and make creator earnings net of expected keeper expense; drive the VRF component from current service settings. A later worktree commit changes the misleading “to keepers” copy, but that change is outside `ec5259c`.

### Security and availability findings

8. **Medium, reasoned: a curated collection can leave a purchased NFT outside the creator's closeout path.** A hostile or paused ERC-721 lets the creator list an NFT, then refuses FWA's delivery `transferFrom` after selection. The verified FWA pool marks it `DeliveryPending`. Permissionless `retryNFTDelivery` only works before the seven-day deadline and still calls the hostile token. After that deadline, only the purchaser can call `refundFailedDelivery`; only after that can the Balancer owner call `recoverRefundedNFT`, which itself still needs the collection to transfer. If the purchaser does nothing or the collection also refuses recovery, the NFT and player escrow remain at FWA beyond closeout. At the launch scale this can tie up the one 0.2 ETH curated NFT and roughly one draw's escrow; a higher-value curated NFT has no small bound. This path was **not executed** because no real hostile curated collection was available without substituting a mock. **Smallest fix:** either scope the closeout guarantee to transferable, purchaser-resolved collections with an explicit operational exception, or integrate an FWA recovery mechanism that can refund on the purchaser's behalf and handle a nontransferable NFT. The existing Balancer alone cannot force the external token to transfer.

## Economic pass: independent map and scorecard

Creator deposits ETH and curated NFTs into the Balancer/FWA pool. The Balancer mints Shapes or Packs: backing is held by those contracts, and mint fees leave that route. Players pay FWA a draw price plus a VRF service fee. FWA escrows draws, pays a fulfilled winner an NFT, accrues owner proceeds and the protocol edge, or credits a refund. Balancer collects owner proceeds, pays keepers from its ETH, records creator profit, and lets the creator claim it or surplus above reserve. Closing stops purchases; outstanding FWA sequences still settle, then the creator can withdraw curated NFTs, unlist and redeem Shapes/Packs, collect proceeds, and claim ETH. Restock fees are embedded in listings and return through won-prize proceeds; unlisted or never-won fees have no player repayment. One-way ratchets: FWA edge, VRF fee, Shapes mint fee, and irreversible retirement. Compare with `docs/SYSTEM-MAP.md` after this independent derivation.

## Things that don't make sense

- The reserve gate passes at opening yet two consecutive 0.5 ETH wins leave the top prize absent for 119 full keeper passes. A reason for the allowed duration and size of a short board is needed.
- The stated keeper-pay outcome uses billed gas, but the on-chain reward prices pre-refund gas. The intended meaning of “gas plus a small flat reward” needs a numeric cap that includes refund-heavy paths.
- A keeper's useful closed-pool reconciliation is promised a reward, while `close()` and `claimSurplus()` permit the creator to remove every wei that could pay it.
- “Winning players repay restock fees” describes a payer distinct from FWA's uniform pre-randomness draw price. The owner needs to choose whether aggregate buyer funding meets the requirement.
- The app calls a pre-VRF amount “Players pay” even though the verified FWA fee adds 0.0117 ETH at the 5 gwei cap. A player-acceptable all-in price has not been stated.
- The closeout receipt balances ETH but leaves a 0.101245503168401864 ETH FWA bucket unsplit among protocol edge, refund and escrow rights. The creator's return cannot be inferred from a balanced sum alone.
